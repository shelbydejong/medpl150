Reading 1 Reflection

When it comes to photography, I find myself searching for the approval of others. My photography is mostly done to capture something which I can then share with the world – usually via instagram – and since I don’t consider it to be something I take a particular interest or pleasure in, that makes it simply a means to an end. Photos, in my life, act as either a way for me to remember something, a way for me to share what I have experienced, or a way for others to experience me. I find it extremely interesting how much Sontag’s writing, from 1977, relates even more so to the world today. With the need to document the world around us feeling ever more pressing, and a culture of “doomscrolling” and short-form content making the world even more saturated with stimulation, I think it is important to capture images that combat that. To elicit a feeling of peace, an opportunity to slow down, to experience something different than the eternal hum of a social media timeline.
Although that may be what I wish to create, in order to represent visually the world at this moment a much different approach is required. I think to depict the oversaturation of this world, the “thickness” as Sontag named it, I would use layers. Collage of images, where nothing is able to be fully seen and perhaps parts of what you should understand is being lost, is a fitting format to reflect the current world. Images of screens, of destruction, immobilization of the individual, is what I would depict.
I don’t engage much in photography as a hobby, when it comes to image making I tend towards painting, drawing, and collage with found images. As with most artists, I began with drawing the face. But the more I attempted to perfectly capture faces as I wanted them to be, to create a style that I thought was “cool”, the more I grew frustrated with the gap between what I wanted to create and what I had the ability to create. I turned to abstract art, in an attempt to evoke emotion rather than depict a literal image, and that is where I have remained. Abstract art feels less constraining to make, and yet it is no less powerful.
I am interested in how this can translate to photography, which is commonly believed to be the most literal and objective way to capture life. After reading Sontag’s piece, though, I realize photography is not nearly as objective as I had previously believed it to be. As with most things humans create, our creations are imbued with our own intentions, beliefs, and biases. I am intrigued to see how I can create my own subjective photos, how I can abstract life as I see it. I also hope to employ some of what Sontag mentioned pertaining to the act of taking a photo. Just as painting can be viewed as a process you can lose yourself in, I want to find a similar feeling in photography.
